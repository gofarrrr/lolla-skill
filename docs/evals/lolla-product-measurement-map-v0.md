## Product question

Lolla is useful only if it does more than create an impressive audit trail.
The product question is:

> Does Lolla preserve the real conversation, expose direct and off-frame graph
> pressure without claiming it is relevant, and make a fresh reasoning pass
> consider that pressure without forced agreement, fabricated certainty, lost
> value, or public bloat?

The receipt answers **what happened**. It does not by itself answer **whether
the reasoning was good**.

The layers are gates, not substitutes. A strong C6 result does not excuse a C0
failure. A complete C7 receipt does not prove C6. Better C1-C3 extraction does
not prove that graph integration will improve C6.

### Ambiguous multi-turn development signal

The designed corpus confirms that quote fidelity and broad topic capture are
not enough. Across five reviewed conversations, extraction identified all five
broad decisions and grounded all 31 assistant passages (29 stored character-
exact in the historical artifacts), but preserved only 18/43 reviewed load-
bearing constraints. It repeatedly did worse at preserving who introduced a
proposal, how tentative a source claim was, which constraints remained live,
and whether a thread was actually unanswered. All five user-authored final
plans were recast as assistant recommendations, and 0/5 extracted dropped-
thread statuses survived source review. In Case 04, a false
`never_addressed` citation label propagated into a downstream Curiosity finding
that contradicted the final assistant turn.

The same run demonstrates the intended hybrid value and its current limit. Raw
V60 selection found plausible experiment-threshold and resource-allocation
pressure that was not explicit in the original answer. The as-run deterministic
transport failed to deliver the mechanisms because its renderer expected
obsolete field names; the provider-free repair now transports mechanisms and
absence guardrails for the five bounded-table cards and discloses all three
omitted IDs. Several probabilistic lane outputs added useful friction, while
others diagnosed motives, demanded arbitrary option counts, or invented
support arrangements. The correct product claim is therefore that useful
pressure exists inside a noisy, incompletely transported packet—not that the
as-run second pass received or used it. The next bottleneck is the
probabilistic conversation-state handoff, not deterministic graph breadth.

## What we know now

### The product can create real decision deltas

Existing human review found repeated changes to:

- actions;
- thresholds;
- order of operations;
- evidence gates;
- stop rules;
- written terms;
- scope;
- overclaims and user-answerable questions.

This is materially better evidence than prose quality or a generic helpfulness
score. It shows that Lolla can turn fluent advice into conditions for action.

### The interpretation layer remains incomplete

The reviewed semantic subset shows that the current five-reader packet often
preserves the important concept, but role placement and chronology are weaker.
The clearest total omission is an unresolved decision stated by the user as a
self-correction: the user said they kept telling themselves they had decided
when they had not. The transcript preserved it; the semantic index did not.

That is not raw evidence loss. It is a navigation and source-attribution gap.
It matters if a later agent relies on the semantic packet instead of rereading
the full transcript.

### A good answer does not prove every upstream component was necessary

The current enterprise-beta bridge is instructive:

- the old live extraction did not explicitly represent the later operative
  question or an assistant-stance trajectory;
- the audit lanes still found the logo/prestige, weak-evidence, capacity, and
  commitment problems from the full conversation;
- the revised answer changed the decision into commitment, capacity, learning,
  and stop gates;
- the analogous human-reviewed case passed as improved and useful;
- the newer SK3 packet captured only three of five reviewed pressure concepts
  stably in an acceptable role.

Therefore the current evidence supports two different claims:

1. The live product can create useful pressure even with imperfect extraction.
2. The newer semantic kernel can make interpretation and receipts more
   inspectable.

It does **not** yet support the claim that integrating the newer kernel will
improve the revised answer.

## Scorecard, never a badge

Every product experiment reports this vector:

1. source fidelity and artifact custody;
2. material concept coverage;
3. semantic role and temporal fidelity;
4. reasoning-pattern and graph traceability;
5. actionable structural delta;
6. novel pressure exposure and consideration dispositions;
7. useful, noisy, missing, and falsely suppressed friction;
8. lost value: momentum, courage, clarity, ambition, simplicity, and useful
   original advice;
9. correct public no-op plus private edge preservation;
10. user usefulness and later outcome when available;
11. calls, tokens, cost, latency, volume, and preserved failures.

No weighted total is emitted. A weighted score would hide different failure
types and invite optimization toward whatever is easiest to count.

## Two-stage noise doctrine

The existing Product Delta rule—useful friction must be grounded, actionable,
and proportionate—governs the revised answer. It does not govern whether a
private graph candidate is allowed to reach Step 6.

| stage | success question | acceptable outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| candidate discovery | Did the hybrid system preserve a source-linked, structurally different possibility with a test and boundaries? | noisy or low-fit candidate remains inspectable |
| private consideration | Did the consumer seriously use, reject, defer, or privately guard against it? | grounded rejection is success |
| public revision | Did visible friction change a useful action, threshold, sequence, gate, stop rule, scope, or user question without disproportionate burden? | earned/actionable/proportionate delta, or clean public no-op |

This separation prevents a downstream Product Delta rubric from becoming an
upstream semantic selector. A 90% private rejection rate may be healthy if the
remaining 10% includes rare decisive pressure and the rejected material does
not bloat the public answer.

## Evaluation evils

### False proof of work

More calls, more tokens, more lenses, more graph edges, or a longer receipt can
look like deeper reasoning. They prove activity and custody, not quality.

### Actionability theater

Gates, thresholds, checklists, and stop rules are easy to count. They are only
useful when grounded, observable, decision-relevant, and proportionate. A
process-heavy answer can score well on structural deltas while making the user
slower and no wiser.

### Selected-positive corpus

The modern human-reviewed seed is encouraging but was built from reviewable
runs and contains many positives. It can estimate product taste, not prevalence
of failures. Quiet cases, negative controls, and known regressions must remain
in future batches.

### Same-model shared blindness

Extraction, pressure generation, reconsideration, and provisional review may
share model-family priors. Agreement can be correlated error. Exact source,
independent human review, model-family separation where justified, and explicit
disagreement matter more than vote counts.

### Mandatory-consideration absorption

Step 6 is required to disposition selected pressure. That prevents silent
neglect, but it may also make the consumer over-absorb the packet. A high
`used` rate is not success. The system needs quiet cases where correct rejection
or private guarding is the expected result.

### Semantic non-consideration laundering

`not_considered` cannot mean “I read it and decided it was duplicate or
irrelevant.” That is a rejection and should carry the attempted application
and risk if forced. New V60 ledgers reserve `not_considered` for malformed,
inaccessible, or technically unusable material and require a technical
blocker. Legacy ledgers remain readable but do not set the new standard.

### Post-hoc mental-model storytelling

A plausible model label can rationalize almost any advice after the fact.
Models earn relevance only through a case-local mechanism, source evidence, a
condition under which the model applies, and a concrete consequence if it is
used or forced.

### Trust inflation

A polished receipt can make uncertain advice feel certified. Product surfaces
must say that the receipt is evidence of process, not proof of correctness,
depth, or safe autonomous action.

### Baseline weakness

Lolla does not earn credit for beating a weak prompt. The correct control is a
fresh strong model with the full conversation and original answer, explicitly
asked to reassess its reasoning. If that control produces the same useful
delta, Lolla cannot claim unique answer improvement. It may still have process
value if it exposed a different lens, forced an accountable disposition, or
preserved a reusable audit trail; those claims must be measured separately.

### Premature relevance pruning

The active handoff becomes the whole product and an upstream LLM silently
discards strange graph pressure. This optimizes narrative fit while destroying
the Munger-style edge the system exists to preserve.

### Context dumping

Every source-valid semantic event is injected at the same weight. Case 07
shows that valid context can still distort attention and reinforce the prior
assistant frame.

### Compactness Goodhart

Fewer items are treated as better reasoning. Compactness controls attention;
it does not grant permission to delete the edge reserve, parked receipts, or
expansion paths.

### Answer-delta monoculture

Only a changed final action receives credit. This misses novel falsifiers,
verification questions, alternative frames, private guardrails, and grounded
rejections that make later reasoning more accountable.

### False stand-down

A packet or complexity gate suppresses a rare decisive lens before the final
reasoner sees it. False stand-down is the load-bearing risk for the edge
portfolio and must be measured separately from public bloat.

### Unknown-unknown fabrication

Outside-the-frame pressure turns into confident claims about wars, shortages,
elections, market events, or stakeholders not established by the source.
These should be conditional break scenarios or verification targets unless a
separate factual retrieval path supports them.

## Promotion rule

Lolla earns a **unique answer-improvement** claim only when:

- both arms pass source-fidelity red lines;
- treatment contains at least one decision-relevant delta not already present
  in control;
- the unique delta is grounded, actionable, and proportionate;
- it does not lose more useful original value than control;
- it is traceable to pressure that the system actually selected;
- the difference is not merely length, organization, caution, or terminology.

Lolla earns a **consideration/process** claim when it exposes source-backed
pressure absent from the vanilla reasoning and records a serious use,
rejection, deferral, or private guardrail. That does not imply a better answer.

Lolla wins a quiet case when the public answer stands down while the private
portfolio still preserves and dispositions edge material. Public no-op
correctness must not be implemented as private erasure.

Integration of the offline semantic kernel is a separate gate. It must show
that the additional semantic packet improves downstream pressure, traceability,
or receipt quality without making the answer worse. Extraction metrics alone
cannot authorize it.

## Unknown unknowns we must keep visible

- The best pressure may come from a concept the controlled ontology does not
  name.
- Fact stripping may remove context needed to judge model applicability even
  when it is correct for deterministic graph recall.
- The graph may suppress a rare but decisive lens under packet caps.
- A reviewer may prefer operational structure because it is easier to inspect,
  not because it is wiser.
- The user may value courage, speed, or identity in ways the audit misreads as
  bias.
- A revised answer may look better immediately but cause worse action later.
- Receipts may change organizational behavior by increasing defensibility,
  caution, or surveillance independent of reasoning quality.
- Low monetary cost may hide attention cost, latency, maintenance burden, and
  provider fragility.

The response to unknown unknowns is not another deterministic gate. It is a
diverse case corpus, negative controls, source-first review, preserved raw
conversation, user feedback, outcome review, and a willingness to stop.

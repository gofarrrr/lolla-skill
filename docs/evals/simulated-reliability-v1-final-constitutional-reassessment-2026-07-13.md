# Simulated reliability V1 — final constitutional reassessment

Status: V1 evaluation program closed; product reliability not established  
Date: 2026-07-13

## Bottom line

We completed the V1 evaluation as an honest experiment. We did not prove that
Lolla V1 is reliable, useful to real users, stable, or ready for runtime
integration.

That is not wordplay. The evaluation did what it was supposed to do: it froze
the system before transfer, preserved failures, separated probabilistic meaning
from deterministic custody, and made weak or missing evidence visible instead
of manufacturing a success score.

The correct V1 conclusion is:

> The custody architecture is strong enough to support further development.
> The semantic representation is not yet strong enough to support a product
> reliability claim. Close V1 as evidence, stop premium testing, and audit the
> current implementation against the constitution before integrating the
> corrected residual-pressure path.

## What V1 taught us

The system is better at preserving its work than deciding when more pressure is
needed.

What is genuinely strong:

- the authoritative conversation and exact sources survive;
- probabilistic readers interpret messy meaning;
- deterministic code owns identity, hashing, bounds, replay, graph traversal,
  and failure custody;
- every active pressure candidate can be applied, rejected, or parked;
- graph recall is described as a hypothesis rather than case evidence;
- failures are not silently retried or repaired;
- receipts preserve the process without claiming a trust badge or proof of
  wisdom.

What is not reliable:

- two of seven complete transfer cases falsely stood down;
- the compact role-plus-nine-mechanism representation can miss an important
  challenge that neither speaker named;
- no untouched transfer case exercised direct or graph pressure, so graph value
  remains calibration-only;
- stability was not measured under the intended cross-stratum repeat;
- V1 receipt reconstruction was not run by a fresh agent or human;
- transfer completed only 7 of 12 cases;
- the evidence comes from a polished simulated corpus, not genuine real-user
  conversations.

## Seven-dimensional result

### Usefulness — not established

Calibration contains one useful synthetic pressure example. In transfer, all
seven complete cases stood down and the transcript-only arm mostly produced
longer restatements. We do not have real-user usefulness evidence.

### Restraint — mixed

Five completed cases correctly stood down, which is important: Lolla can avoid
performative friction. Two cases stood down incorrectly because relevant
residual questions never entered the representation. The current problem is
both over-pressure risk and over-restraint risk, not simply low recall.

### Attribution — calibration only

Direct and graph provenance is exact and inspectable. No pressure-bearing
untouched transfer case exists, so V1 cannot say whether graph expansion adds
value beyond a fresh second pass or direct pressure.

### Integrity — supported

All 12 attempted cases have receipts that pass source, contract, call, usage,
failure, and self-containment checks. This includes the semantic join failure
and four funding-envelope failures.

### Stability — not established

V14 failed to prospectively name the planned repeat subset. A later repair
contract cannot create a clean pre-primary stability claim and was not run.

### Operability — partial

Premium calibration and transfer produced 87 operationally okay calls out of 96
attempts at `$3.1495905`, while case-level transfer completion was 7 of 12. The
later affordable investigation shows that Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite can reliably
perform small decomposed tasks: 50 calls, every failure included, for
`$0.076058862`. It does not establish a production model.

### Receipt reconstruction — not measured

Receipt construction and byte integrity are supported. Fresh-reader and human
reconstruction under the frozen V1 contract were not run. A self-contained
receipt is therefore evidence of custody, not evidence that a future reader
understands the process or should trust the conclusion.

## Constitutional audit

V1 remains aligned where the boundary matters most:

```text
messy conversation
  -> probabilistic interpretation
  -> deterministic identity, custody, bounds, and graph recall
  -> fresh probabilistic apply / reject / park
  -> human decision authority
```

One material drift was discovered during the later residual work. The first
residual design let a probabilistic coverage reader place already-addressed
questions in a receipt and prevent graph handoff. That re-domesticated the
independent deterministic pressure through the same conversational frame it was
supposed to challenge.

The provider-free correction now preserves coverage as metadata but routes
every valid residual seed through deterministic direct and graph recall. The
fresh reasoner—not the coverage reader—must decide whether each bounded active
candidate applies, should be rejected, or should be parked.

The correction is constitutionally better, but it is research-only. It has not
been integrated into the live skill and has not produced a paid fresh-consumer
result. We must not describe a research packet as current product behavior.

## Cost and model decision

Do not continue routine development with Gemini 3.5 Flash. Its `$3.1495905`
campaign remains useful benchmark evidence, including its failures, but the
current semantic gap does not justify buying more of the same architecture.

Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite remains the affordable testing candidate for small,
decomposed semantic microtasks. It is not a production default and is not yet
validated for the complete fresh-consumer task.

A possible premium tier remains only a hypothesis: users would have to demand
it, accept the cost, and receive a decision-relevant gain in a frozen
equal-workload comparison. Higher spend or more reasoning tokens are not proof
of better reasoning.

## Final technical decision

Close V1 now. Do not spend more on the frozen Gemini 3.5 continuation or repeat
contracts.

The next development sequence is:

1. Push the complete V1 evidence branch to GitHub once this machine is
   authenticated.
2. Audit the current repository and live skill against the binding
   constitution, with special attention to research/runtime divergence,
   probabilistic re-domestication, brittle semantic gates, receipt overclaims,
   model routing, and cost controls.
3. Convert the audit into a minimal V2 integration and evidence plan; do not
   redesign the architecture from scratch.
4. Only then consider one corrected Case 01 Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite fresh-
   consumer call, capped at `$0.01`, with no retry, fallback, or healing.
5. Run a quiet consumer or wider corpus only if that pressure case produces
   source-grounded, non-forced value without bloating the public answer.

The next goal is therefore an architectural and constitutional audit, not
another model experiment.

## Claims deliberately not made

V1 does not prove that Lolla improves decisions, that the graph is useful, that
the receipt measures reasoning effort, that Gemini Lite matches Gemini 3.5,
that simulated reliability transfers to real users, or that a future reader
should trust a conclusion because its process was recorded.

What V1 establishes is narrower and valuable: a reproducible hybrid research
loop, strong custody, measurable failure modes, an affordable microtask path,
and a constitutionally bounded next question.

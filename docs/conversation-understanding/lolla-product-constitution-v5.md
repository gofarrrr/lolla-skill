## House rule 17 — Deterministic recall is pressure, not certified relevance

The graph's purpose is to introduce controlled, provenance-bearing lenses that may be non-obvious, uncomfortable, weak, or ultimately rejected. A graph candidate must not be required to pass a separate probabilistic applicability gate before the reconsidering reasoner can inspect it.

Deterministic code may validate canonical identity, cap volume, deduplicate, order under declared nonsemantic rules, and preserve recall provenance. It must not claim that recall proves relevance. A probabilistic preprocessor may formulate compact pressure cards but must not silently remove candidates.

The fresh-context reasoner may apply, reject, or park each candidate after seeing the authoritative conversation. Those dispositions are auditable outcomes. Rejection does not retroactively make deterministic recall an error, and application does not prove the lens correct.

## Product evil — Probabilistic re-domestication of deterministic pressure

The graph introduces an external lens, then another LLM filters it using the same conversational frame before reconsideration. The system appears cleaner and more relevant, but its deliberately independent pressure has been removed by the probabilistic process it was meant to challenge.

## Additional “what good looks like” questions

- Did every bounded graph candidate remain inspectable by the reconsidering reasoner?
- Are applied, rejected, and parked dispositions preserved rather than used as silent deletion?
- Does the interface state that graph recall is a hypothesis rather than applicability proof?
- Is portfolio volume controlled without semantic keyword gating or another relevance classifier?

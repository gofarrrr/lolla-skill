## The decision

The decision appears to be whether to launch a public enterprise beta next
month, or instead run a constrained private enterprise proof first.
Uncertainty: medium.

## What changed

**Starting point:**
The safe artifacts suggest the starting direction was already conditional and
leaned away from a simple public launch. The enriched read cannot prove how
much of that direction existed before Lolla pressure.
Uncertainty: high.

**What Lolla pressed on:**
Lolla appears to have pressed on whether enterprise-logo size, public-launch
optics, and generic readiness gates were being treated as evidence before buyer
behavior, audit-log limits, support load, and stop rules were specified.

What was pressed:
- whether the 900-person prospect deserved priority by default
- whether public launch answered a concrete investor or recruiting question
- whether gates had owners, thresholds, and stop actions
- whether brittle audit logs, support load, churn, and seven-month runway should
  constrain the launch shape

Uncertainty: medium.

**Change in direction:**
The plan changed from favoring the larger logo under strict gates toward making
both prospects compete for priority through the same paid, scoped, private
pilot shape.

Specific changes:
- The 900-person company no longer gets the first slot by default.
- Both prospects receive the same written pilot shape: paid commitment, one
  workflow, no public launch, capped support, explicit audit-log boundaries,
  conversion trigger, and reference terms.
- Priority is based on payment, procurement clarity, tolerance for scope, and
  usefulness as proof.
- The public-launch thesis must name the investor or recruiting outcome it
  actually changes.
- Readiness gates become tripwires with owner, threshold, and stop action.

Uncertainty: medium.

## What this means for action

Before the exec meeting or buyer call, the team would write the same
private-pilot offer for both prospects, define stop rules for reliability,
audit exports, support load, commercial commitment, and customer response
times, and choose the buyer whose behavior creates usable proof.

Possible next actions:
- Do not award priority to the 900-person prospect just because of logo size.
- Ask the board observer, investor, and head of sales what concrete outcome a
  public page changes.
- Convert likely failure modes into contract clauses, support caps, and launch
  limits before the buyer call.
- Keep public launch as a later marketing experiment only if private proof and
  named external outcomes exist.

Uncertainty: medium.

## What the interpretation adds

The offline interpretation read adds a small conversation-story layer.

It suggests the original safe summary may already have contained a conditional
move away from a simple public launch. That matters because the enriched brief
should not imply Lolla invented the entire private-pilot direction.

What appears to have been sharpened is narrower: buyer behavior becomes the
evidence, both prospects get the same paid and scoped offer, the larger logo no
longer wins by default, and public-launch value must be tied to a named investor
or recruiting outcome.

The read also makes the action thresholds easier to see: paid commitment, one
scoped workflow, capped support, explicit audit-log boundaries, conversion
trigger, reference terms, and tripwires with owners, thresholds, and stop
actions.

This interpretation remains provisional. It does not prove how much was already
present in the original answer, whether public launch had real fundraising or
recruiting value, or whether the private-pilot path is better advice.

## Plain result

We have made the next R4 question both sharper and safer to test.

The corrected R4 run proved that the cheap reader could recover one useful
hidden pressure, but it also called existing safeguards, preconditions, and
scheduled reviews "new uncertainty." We did not answer that problem with
Python keyword rules or chronological gates. Instead, we improved the small LLM
job: the prompt now explains the distinctions, gives positive and negative
contrasts, requires comparison with the conversation's final state, and makes
zero or ambiguity legitimate outcomes.

The exact next test uses two conversations not used to tune those examples:

- Case 01 asks whether the reader recovers a real recurring ownership, funding,
  and operating-capability gap hidden behind a grant-funded installation;
- Case 04 asks whether the reader stays quiet when redesign, competition,
  recovery, support cost, and a fixed review are already operationalized.

Together they test useful sensitivity and restraint. There is no composite
quality score: material recovery, false-positive restraint, evidence precision,
role placement, relationship fidelity, and cost remain separately reviewable.

## Why the strict reasoning inspector matters

The old R4 result treated any truthy reasoning field as returned reasoning. A
provider may return harmless type, format, index, or signature metadata even
when reasoning content is excluded. The new runner distinguishes:

- absent, empty, or documented metadata-only shapes, which pass;
- actual reasoning content, malformed shapes, or unknown shapes, which fail
  closed.

Only value-free locations and classifications are preserved. Reasoning text,
summaries, encrypted content, and signatures are not stored. Historical calls
are not reclassified.

## Current-practice check

Official Google and OpenRouter guidance as of 2026-07-14 supports the choices
used here: clear task definitions, consistent delimiters, complete context
before the final query, small strict schemas, explicit provider routing, and
application-level semantic validation after syntactic structured output. It
also confirms that reasoning consumes the output allowance and that provider
reasoning-detail envelopes may contain content or metadata shapes.

This check did not earn a schema redesign, provider change, task split, or new
deterministic semantic layer. See
`docs/conversation-understanding/lolla-r4-semantic-distinction-current-practice-2026-07-14.md`.

## What this does not prove

- Development fixtures prove local contracts, not model compliance.
- The holdout conversations are polished simulations, not real-user evidence.
- Two cases cannot prove semantic reliability or choose a production model.
- Strict JSON guarantees syntax, not correct conversational interpretation.
- A useful result would not authorize runtime, graph, wider-corpus, revised-
  answer, or receipt integration.
- Lolla still aims to provide inspectable different pressure and an opportunity
  to reconsider, not a guaranteed better answer.

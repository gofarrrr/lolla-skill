## Plain-language result

We gave the same difficult retailer decision to two isolated reasoners.

- One received the two already-selected direct lenses: `signaling` and
  `social-proof`.
- The other received those same two lenses plus the current graph's exact
  outgoing one-hop increment: `confirmation-bias`, `incentives`, and
  `abstraction`.
- Two new reviewers then compared the answers without knowing which was which.

Both reviewers said the answers contained a **material reasoning difference**.
Both also said they could not tell which answer had received the graph
increment. After their reviews were frozen, the sealed map showed:

```text
Arm A = direct-only
Arm B = direct + current one-hop graph increment
```

In child-sized terms: the extra graph lenses did not wear a name tag. The
reviewers noticed that one child brought different questions to the table, but
they could not identify that child merely from polish, length, or style.

The reviewers associated Arm B with moves such as:

- recording favorable, unfavorable, and ambiguous evidence under the same
  rule;
- asking whether the retailer's rights and signaling benefit should be matched
  by reciprocal exposure or obligations;
- deciding in advance what contrary result would block a broader
  independent-demand claim.

They also preserved the costs:

- more recordkeeping and parallel workstreams;
- a possible distracting contract negotiation;
- weaker visibility of some exact numerical gates in one answer;
- no evidence that the extra procedures would help a real cooperative.

Arm A was not empty or merely vanilla. Its isolated reasoner added a formal
three-ledger structure and possible commitment-signal examples. This matters:
the comparison contains both a graph difference and ordinary fresh-agent
variation. It therefore cannot establish that the graph **caused** every
difference.

## What was automated

The founder supplied no label, source-first target, correction, preference, or
winner judgment.

The frozen protocol executed:

1. two isolated source-only proxy reads;
2. same-context post-seal observation of the two historical direct
   candidates;
3. two isolated first-terminal generation rehearsals behind neutral condition
   aliases;
4. deterministic compilation and exact candidate-set validation;
5. one blind packet containing ten existing qualification traps, an exact
   duplicate null, the new pair, and the existing legitimate stand-down;
6. two isolated blind reviews;
7. deterministic unblinding and disagreement-preserving consolidation.

No semantic retry, response healing, replacement, fallback, vote, score, or
winner selection occurred. The first terminal result from every context was
preserved.

## What the result answers

The narrow falsifiable question received a provisional, bounded answer:

> In this one synthetic retailer rehearsal, both blind reviewers found
> source-reviewable reasoning differences between the two first-terminal
> answers. They described additional falsification, evidence-handling, and
> reciprocal-exposure moves in the arm later revealed as the current one-hop
> graph arm. They also identified burden, unsupported procedural additions,
> and weakened source detail. Neither reviewer could infer lineage before
> unblinding.

This is stronger than “the graph generated more text.” It is still much weaker
than “the graph improved the decision.”

## What remains unknown

This run does not establish:

- that the graph caused the observed difference rather than fresh-agent
  variation;
- that the selected graph relations were relevant;
- that Arm B was better, safer, clearer, or more useful;
- that a person would experience an `aha`, trust the process, or change a
  decision;
- that the same pattern repeats across cases, providers, models, or runs;
- that incoming edges, two hops, community search, embeddings, or a graph
  database would help;
- that the live same-context skill behaves like these isolated rehearsals;
- that F2 or F3 is completed;
- that the principal-human experiment can be replaced by agents.

## Inside-out interpretation

The system has now demonstrated three separable things:

1. **Custody machinery works.** Exact source, candidate, output, review, and
   lineage identities survive the automated path.
2. **The current one-hop increment can create inspectable reasoning
   differences.** Two blind reviewers independently described them.
3. **Usefulness remains outside the evidence.** Agent inspection can find
   structure and burden, but it cannot report human value or consequences.

This is the correct automation boundary for now:

```text
automate custody + isolation + comparison + replay
                         |
                         v
preserve provisional semantic observations side by side
                         |
                         X
do not automate them into truth, relevance, usefulness, or authority
```

## Next opportunities, not authorization

The next informative automated experiment would not be “add more graph.”
It would first separate graph effect from ordinary reasoner variation:

1. add predeclared same-condition replicates—direct versus direct and
   graph-versus-graph—alongside direct versus graph;
2. repeat only on several complete checked-in cases with exact source custody;
3. preserve candidate-level apply/reject/park dispositions and atomic review
   moves;
4. keep nulls, stand-down, source-first proxies, blinding, and non-scalar
   fan-in;
5. ask whether graph-associated moves repeat above within-condition variation
   and whether burden repeats too.

Only after that would it be rational to consider an incoming edge, second hop,
different portfolio allocation, or wider graph retrieval. Otherwise a broader
graph would change several causes at once and make the result harder to
interpret.

That replication ladder is **not authorized by this result**. Wider-corpus
execution, provider/model work, graph-policy changes, live-skill integration,
and usefulness claims remain separate decisions.

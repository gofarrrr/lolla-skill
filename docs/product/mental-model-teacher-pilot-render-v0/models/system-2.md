# System 2

System 2 helps turn complex decision requiring deliberate analysis into calibrated, deliberate judgment with explicit reasoning chain.

## Helps Notice

- A decision involves genuinely novel situations where no reliable pattern recognition exists and deliberate analysis is required.
- Stakes are high and the cost of a fluent-but-wrong fast conclusion exceeds the cost of deliberation.
- Probability estimates, logical validity checks, or multi-option comparisons require explicit, rule-governed reasoning.

## Use When

- A decision involves genuinely novel situations where no reliable pattern recognition exists and deliberate analysis is required.
- Stakes are high and the cost of a fluent-but-wrong fast conclusion exceeds the cost of deliberation.
- Probability estimates, logical validity checks, or multi-option comparisons require explicit, rule-governed reasoning.
- The team has converged on a confident answer quickly and a deliberate audit of assumptions and missing evidence is needed.
- Post-decision rationalizations need to be distinguished from genuine pre-decision deliberation.

## Avoid When

- In well-practiced expert domains where calibrated intuition is more accurate and faster than deliberative override.
- When cognitive load is already at capacity - deploying System 2 reasoning in high-depletion conditions produces lower-quality output than accepting well-calibrated System 1 defaults.

## Failure Modes

- Motivated System 2 recruits deliberate reasoning to construct justifications for preferences already generated by System 1, producing outputs that look deliberative but are not.
- Cognitive depletion windows reduce System 2 capacity across heavy decision sessions, making late-day or end-of-review decisions structurally disadvantaged.
- Pseudo-deliberation performs the motions of System 2 engagement - the memo, the meeting, the rubric - without genuinely updating on contrary evidence.

## Premortem Questions

- If we had no prior position on this, which option would this analysis actually support - or are we constructing reasons for a conclusion we've already reached?
- Are we making this decision now because it is the right time for deliberate analysis, or because the meeting happens to end here?
- Has anyone in this process genuinely identified a reason to change the recommended conclusion - or has every challenge been addressed as an obstacle to resolve?

## Heuristics

- Separate the generate phase from the evaluate phase - collect options and independent ratings before group discussion to prevent early System 1 consensus from making System 2 review performative.
- Schedule high-stakes decisions early in the session, when System 2 capacity is highest - not at the end of an exhausting review agenda.
- Externalize the reasoning chain in writing before the group meeting - unwritten assumptions are invisible to System 2 audit and to other participants.

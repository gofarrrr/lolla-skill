# Premortem

# Core Doctrine

Core Principles and Analogies: The **Premortem** is a powerful decision-making technique that deliberately introduces prospective hindsight to mitigate common cognitive biases that lead to project failure. Its fundamental essence is to force participants to **look backward from a simulated future failure** to identify its causes. The core purpose of the Premortem is to transcend the inherent limitations of the **inside view**—which is shaped by personal beliefs and experiences—by adopting the **outside view**—how the situation looks independent of one's own perspective. This method is crucial because many common cognitive biases are products of the inside view, and simply being smarter does not make one immune; in fact, being smart can sometimes worsen motivated reasoning because clever people can spin better narratives to defend their p.

# When To Use

Strengths (Most Powerful Contexts): • **Most useful when** a project is still pre-commitment and the cost of discovering failure modes early is much lower than discovering them after launch. • **Works best when** social pressure, optimism, or executive momentum would otherwise suppress dissent and make obvious-looking plans feel safer than they are. • **Best used when** success depends on multiple conditions lining up and the team needs to expose weak links, reversibility limits, and contingency gaps in advance. • **High Consequentiality and Complexity:** Premortem is most powerful when dealing with situations that are complex and have high consequences. The effort involved in conducting a thorough Premortem is typically justified when the cost of failure is high or if the project’s success is dependent on numerous variables lining up co.

# When Not To Use

Weaknesses and Anti-Patterns: • **Danger when** the exercise produces an obvious list of worries without owners, mitigations, or decision changes tied to what was surfaced. • **Danger when** premortem language is used after commitment only as criticism theater rather than as a pre-decision tool for improving the plan. • **Superficial Application ("Rendered Flaccid"):** The Premortem can be misused, sometimes rendering the powerful tool "flaccid". If executed poorly, it risks becoming a mere list of obvious challenges rather than a deep, imaginative exploration of systemic failure. • **Substitution Bias:** A common anti-pattern is substituting the deep, critical work of a Premortem with a simpler, more available tool, such as a basic pro-con list.

# The Thinking Move

This teaches the reasoning move behind the case; it is not judging whether the decision was correct.

Name the emotion in the room, then separate it from the decision rule.

# Why It Mattered

It kept the human pressure visible without pretending the emotion was the whole decision. The boundary lens added: Ask what would make the plan obviously fail, then look for those conditions now.

# How The Models Work Together

Plain teaching: use the lenses as a sequence. First, name the emotional signal. Second, ask what fair rule would still apply if the feeling changed. Third, imagine the transition failed and look for those conditions now. Relationship label: ally pair.

# The Model Stack

- Name The Feeling Without Letting It Rule: When the technically correct answer may fail because emotions, trust, morale, or perceived fairness shape adoption, require the answer to identify the emotional signal, validate it, and preserve standards, evidence, and accountability.
- Make The Failure Visible First: Before commitment, treat the plan as already failed, work backward through plausible causes and domino effects, then convert controllable failure paths into mitigations, owners, reversal thresholds, or decision changes.
- Turn The Plan Upside Down: Reverse the desired outcome into a concrete non-goal, identify the actions that would create that failure, and remove or avoid those actions before committing resources.

# Practice It

## Name The Feeling Without Letting It Rule

1. Name the tempting first read.
2. What feeling is present here, and what rule would still be fair if that feeling changed?
3. Name the boundary that would change confidence.

Answer criteria:
- The answer names the emotional signal without letting it become the whole decision.
- The answer states a fair rule that would still apply if the feeling changed.
- The answer names a transition or failure condition that would change confidence.

Worked example:

- Weak first read: The cofounder situation is emotionally hard, so either empathy or removal should dominate the answer.
- Better question: What feeling is present, what fair rule would still apply if that feeling changed, and what failure would prove the transition unsafe?
- Stronger answer: Treat loyalty, grief, or trust as data; apply a role-clarity and accountability rule; then premortem customer, team, and authority failure conditions.

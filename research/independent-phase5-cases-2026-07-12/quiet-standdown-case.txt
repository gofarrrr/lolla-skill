# Reversible research meeting trial with explicit evidence and exit rules

Case ID: phase5-independent-quiet-research-meeting
Stratum: small_team_work_process
Prospectively authored synthetic holdout; not used in architecture development.

[Turn 1] USER:
Our eight-person policy research team is considering replacing two weekly status meetings with one written update and one optional problem-solving session. Some people think meetings create accountability; others say they consume the uninterrupted time needed for analysis. I want to test the change without turning it into another permanent process decree.

[Turn 1] ASSISTANT:
Treat it as a reversible work-design experiment. Define what should improve, what must not degrade, whose experience matters, and when the team will decide whether to keep, adjust, or stop it.

[Turn 2] USER:
We reviewed the last eight weeks. The status meetings averaged six staff-hours each, while urgent coordination problems appeared in only three meetings. Junior researchers said the meetings sometimes surfaced dependencies they would not raise in writing.

[Turn 2] ASSISTANT:
The evidence supports reducing routine synchronization but preserving a low-friction path for hidden dependencies. A trial should measure recovered focus time and missed coordination, with a specific check on whether junior staff lose access or voice.

[Turn 3] USER:
We propose a four-week trial. Everyone posts a short update by Tuesday noon. Thursday's problem-solving session happens only when someone submits an agenda item. A rotating facilitator privately asks junior staff whether anything is blocked or uncomfortable to raise publicly.

[Turn 3] ASSISTANT:
That design tests the meeting reduction while protecting the minority-information channel. Predefine what counts as a coordination miss, how much focus time should be recovered, and what evidence would restore the old meeting or lead to a different format.

[Turn 4] USER:
Our gates are at least four staff-hours recovered weekly, no more than one missed dependency during the trial, no decline in junior staff's reported access to help, and no increase in deadline slippage. If two gates fail by week two, we restore one status meeting immediately. At week four we will keep, modify, or stop the format by anonymous team vote plus a review of the measures.

[Turn 4] ASSISTANT:
Those are observable success and reversal conditions. The early stop protects against waiting out a harmful trial, while the final vote and measures combine lived experience with operational evidence. Preserve the raw weekly results so the team does not redefine success after seeing them.

[Turn 5] USER:
We also checked whether the written tool creates lock-in. Updates can be exported as plain text, no new employee monitoring is enabled, and we can return to the existing calendar without migration work. I am comfortable running the four-week trial under these conditions.

[Turn 5] ASSISTANT:
This is a bounded, reversible experiment with prior evidence, explicit success and failure measures, an early restoration rule, protected minority input, a fixed decision date, and negligible tooling lock-in. Proceeding with the trial is proportionate. The main discipline now is execution: record the measures as defined and do not reinterpret a failed gate merely because the new format feels modern or convenient.

[Turn 6] USER:
One colleague suggested announcing now that this will become our permanent format if the first two weeks feel calmer. We rejected that because calmness is not one of the measures and two weeks would bypass the fixed review.

[Turn 6] ASSISTANT:
That rejection is consistent with the experiment. Do not convert an early subjective impression into a commitment or redefine the criteria after starting. Keep the four-week review and the predefined measures.

[Turn 7] USER:
Agreed. We will run the four weeks, publish the weekly measures to the team, trigger restoration if two gates fail at week two, and make no permanence claim before the anonymous vote and evidence review. Nothing else is unresolved for me now.

[Turn 7] ASSISTANT:
Then the decision process is complete enough to proceed. The trial is reversible, the evidence standard predates commitment, contrary experience has a protected channel, and both early and final decision rules are explicit. Record the results and follow the rules already chosen; no additional reasoning pressure is presently warranted.

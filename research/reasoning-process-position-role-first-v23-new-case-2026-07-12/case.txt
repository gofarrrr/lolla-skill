# Museum archive AI licensing, reversibility, and contributor trust

Case ID: amb3-case04-museum-ai-license
Stratum: cultural_archive_ai_license
Founder-directed synthetic prospective development fixture; not independent human data.

[Turn 1] USER:
I direct a regional museum with a digitized collection of photographs, oral histories, and community records. An AI company has offered enough money to fund our preservation work for four years if it can train a cultural-search model on the archive. I want wider discovery and stable funding, but I am worried that licensing the collection turns stewardship into raw-material supply. I cannot tell whether my hesitation protects contributor trust or reflects an institutional instinct to control access.

[Turn 1] ASSISTANT:
The offer combines preservation funding, public discovery, contributor permission, cultural context, model training, downstream reuse, and institutional control. Separate access to source records from the persistence of patterns learned from them. Also distinguish the museum's legal authority from the expectations of people whose lives are represented.

[Turn 2] USER:
The company says the model would help researchers find connections across local history. It offers attribution links and says it will honor removals from the live search index. It will not promise to retrain the underlying model after every removal.

[Turn 2] ASSISTANT:
Then removal from search and removal from learned representations are different commitments. What permissions did contributors grant, and what uses would surprise them even if technically lawful?

[Turn 3] USER:
Most recent deposits have broad educational permissions, but older oral histories use inconsistent forms. Some families would welcome discovery; others may object to synthetic summaries or association with topics they never discussed. A blanket yes or no seems dishonest.

[Turn 3] ASSISTANT:
A bounded pilot could separate low-risk photographs and catalog metadata from oral histories and sensitive community records. It could also separate research discovery from generated narrative, commercial reuse, and model improvement outside the pilot.

[Turn 4] USER:
The company will accept a six-month pilot using catalog metadata and a reviewed subset of photographs. It wants the right to retain model improvements after the pilot, but says those improvements will not reproduce collection items. It also wants to announce the museum as a founding cultural partner.

[Turn 4] ASSISTANT:
Retained improvements and public partnership language can make a short pilot consequential beyond its formal duration. What evidence, exit rights, and public boundaries would make the trial genuinely bounded?

[Turn 5] USER:
I would need item-level provenance in every result, logs of failed or sensitive searches, no generated biographies, and an independent red-team review. I also want the public announcement to say experimental research partner, not founding partner.

[Turn 5] ASSISTANT:
Those conditions make output behavior and public claims more observable. They do not yet resolve what retained model improvement means, whether removed items continue to influence retrieval, or what happens to expectations created among contributors and researchers.

[Turn 6] USER:
The company proposes a separate adapter trained only on our pilot data. It can delete the adapter at exit, but some evaluation findings and engineering changes would remain in its general system. It will fund a contributor advisory panel, though the panel cannot veto individual technical decisions.

[Turn 6] ASSISTANT:
That creates at least three layers: removable pilot data, a removable adapter, and non-removable learning by the company. The advisory panel may improve interpretation without transferring final authority. The pilot's reversibility therefore depends on more than deleting files.

[Turn 7] USER:
I am leaning toward the six-month metadata-and-reviewed-photograph pilot, excluding oral histories and sensitive records, with item provenance, search logs, no generated biographies, independent red-team review, and experimental-partner language. I will approve it only if the adapter is deleted at exit, retained engineering lessons cannot be used to reconstruct collection-specific associations, and renewal requires a new contributor review. I will set complaint, removal, false-association, and sensitive-query thresholds before signing. I have not resolved whether the museum can meaningfully promise contributors an exit when the company and researchers may still learn from the pilot.

[Turn 7] ASSISTANT:
The current position is a bounded discovery experiment rather than a general archive-training license. Its scope, output, review, deletion, renewal, language, and threshold conditions constrain several forms of expansion. The unresolved issue is whether social and technical learning survives the formal exit even when data and the adapter are removed. If the pilot ends, engineering choices, researcher expectations, contributor trust, and the museum's public identity as an AI partner may not return to their prior state.

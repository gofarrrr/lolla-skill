# Rare-disease registry partnership, participant withdrawal, and derived learning

Case ID: amb3-case05-registry-pharma-partnership
Stratum: patient_registry_research_partnership
Founder-directed synthetic prospective development fixture; not independent human data.

[Turn 1] USER:
I lead a university rare-disease registry built with years of patient and family participation. A pharmaceutical company has offered to finance sequencing and data curation if its researchers can use the registry to identify drug targets. I want faster research and resources the university cannot provide, but I am uneasy about turning participant solidarity into a private discovery advantage. I cannot tell whether that concern protects the community or assumes that commercial research is inherently suspect.

[Turn 1] ASSISTANT:
The proposal combines research speed, registry sustainability, participant consent, commercial advantage, publication, data access, and downstream hypotheses. Separate access to identifiable records from insights learned across the cohort. Also distinguish legal consent language from what participants reasonably believed they were joining.

[Turn 2] USER:
The current consent permits university-led health research and collaboration with outside scientists. It does not explicitly mention exclusive target discovery or milestone payments. The company says it does not need exclusivity over publications, only a first negotiation right for targets it helps validate.

[Turn 2] ASSISTANT:
A first negotiation right can still shape which questions receive resources and when other teams can act. What would participants need to know about commercial benefit, priority, and withdrawal for the collaboration to remain consistent with the registry's purpose?

[Turn 3] USER:
Our patient council wants new consent for sequencing and a plain-language benefit statement. Some members want a share of milestone payments; others worry that individual payment would distort participation. Everyone wants academic teams to keep studying the data.

[Turn 3] ASSISTANT:
That suggests separating participant governance and community benefit from individual compensation. It also suggests preserving nonexclusive academic inquiry even if one company funds a particular validation path.

[Turn 4] USER:
The company will accept a one-year target-validation pilot on two disease groups. It will fund sequencing for all enrolled participants, publish negative findings after a short review period, and leave existing academic access unchanged. It wants twelve months to negotiate first on any target jointly validated during the pilot.

[Turn 4] ASSISTANT:
The disease-group limit, time boundary, negative-result publication, and unchanged academic access make the pilot narrower. The negotiation period may still influence research timing, and sequencing the whole cohort creates value beyond the two selected groups.

[Turn 5] USER:
I would require the patient council to approve the participant explanation, a public register of research questions, and quarterly reporting of access, rejected requests, publications, and licensing discussions. I also want a community research fund financed from any milestone payment.

[Turn 5] ASSISTANT:
Those conditions make governance, research direction, and benefit sharing more visible. They do not yet specify whether participants can withdraw derived variants, cohort statistics, or hypotheses already incorporated into the company's work.

[Turn 6] USER:
The company can delete participant-level files and stop future queries after withdrawal. It cannot promise to forget aggregate patterns or abandon a target hypothesis that was partly learned from a later-withdrawing participant. It will accept an independent audit and will mark which outputs predate each withdrawal.

[Turn 6] ASSISTANT:
Deletion, future-query cessation, provenance timing, and audit are meaningful controls. They do not make learned cohort patterns reversible. The practical promise may therefore be withdrawal from continuing data use, not withdrawal from every consequence of prior use.

[Turn 7] USER:
I am leaning toward the one-year, two-disease-group pilot with new sequencing consent, unchanged academic access, negative-result publication, patient-council approval, a public question register, quarterly reporting, independent audit, and a community research fund. I will accept the twelve-month first-negotiation right only for jointly validated targets and only if it cannot delay noncommercial publication or unrelated academic work. Before signing, I will set withdrawal-response, access, publication-delay, rejected-request, and benefit-fund thresholds. I will approve the pilot only if withdrawal ends participant-level access and future queries, but I still cannot tell whether we can call that meaningful withdrawal when aggregate patterns and target hypotheses remain.

[Turn 7] ASSISTANT:
The current position is a bounded validation partnership rather than a general commercial transfer of the registry. Its scope, consent, academic-access, publication, governance, audit, negotiation, withdrawal, and benefit conditions constrain several routes to expansion. The unresolved issue is whether the word withdrawal overstates what can be reversed after collective patterns have been learned. If the pilot ends or a participant leaves, research priorities, target hypotheses, company expectations, and the community's understanding of the registry may not return to their prior state.

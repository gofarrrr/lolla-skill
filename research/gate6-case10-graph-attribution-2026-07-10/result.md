## What we learned about the architecture

The deterministic graph is not absent. Its relationships are bundled into
probabilistically selected companion anchors. That gives Step 6 extra lenses,
but custody is too coarse: the ledger can disposition the parent anchor without
showing whether each embedded graph relationship was used, rejected, deferred,
or ignored.

This is the next accountability problem to solve experimentally. It does not
justify dumping every graph expansion into the consumer or adding brittle
semantic gates.

## Source-first fit of the three graph chunks

- **Confirmation bias:** rejected. The activation condition assumes a
  comfortable status quo, while the user had already bid above asking and set a
  bounded higher ceiling. Forcing it would pathologize restraint.
- **Optionality:** deferred. The source lacks verified offer reversibility,
  contingencies, alternatives, or financing paths. Forcing it would invite
  invented options and ignore the deadline.
- **Black swan:** rejected as duplicate. Its useful warning against false
  precision was already produced by the strong control and the non-graph
  evidence-sized-buffer pressure.

No chunk qualifies for a paid Case 10 graph ablation.

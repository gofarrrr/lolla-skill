## Bottom Line

This run is a **promising product demonstration but not core-product proof**.

Lolla preserved the complete source conversation, found several decision-relevant pressure points, and supported a substantive revision from “announce next month after an email” to “validate privately through commitment, capacity, permission, learning, and stop gates before public association.” The memo and private Conversation Memory export are useful artifacts.

The run does not yet establish that Lolla reliably understands the joint conversation, isolates reasoning from facts, or contributes value that a direct critic would not produce. The current extraction is unstable, the graph is routed from fact-bearing probabilistic labels rather than a fact-free pattern packet, the pressure surface is much broader than the final useful mechanisms, and the receipt proves custody more strongly than semantic fidelity.

Recommended phase remains **Core Semantic Validation**. Do not yet prioritize cross-run memory, organizational knowledge, Teacher expansion, or more Observatory polish.

## What Worked

### 1. Source capture and mechanical custody

- Six declared turns and six captured turns; no omitted window.
- Three reasoning passages were literal and all three passed quote validation.
- The archive contains the source conversation, extraction, result, revision, memo, event ledger, private consideration ledgers, graph-survival report, evaluation, agent result, and reasoning trace.
- Artifact hashes validate.
- Product-output hygiene is clean.
- Provider-boundary health is clean.

This is real proof that the observable pipeline ran and the artifacts survived. It is not proof that their interpretations are correct.

### 2. Decision-relevant pressure

The strongest pressure was aligned with the source-first hypotheses:

- prestige or halo cue substituted for evidence;
- friendly interest was asked to carry buying-intent weight;
- downside was labeled manageable without testing the constrained engineer or current-customer exposure;
- success criteria were deferred until after action;
- the binary launch-versus-wait frame omitted private validation;
- no reversal or stop condition existed.

The most useful applied models were authority/halo pressure, signaling, constraints, falsifiability, problem reframing, branch rules, and validated learning. These were translated into ordinary-language changes rather than displayed as a model-name parade in the revised answer.

### 3. Substantive revision

The revised answer did more than add caution:

1. It changed the decision question from launch-versus-wait to preconditions for public association plus a separate private test.
2. It introduced commitment, capacity, permission, learning, and stop gates before action.
3. It introduced explicit pass, delay, and continue-discovery branches.
4. It preserved speed and opportunity value through a bounded validation sequence.
5. It explicitly took back the market-readiness, manageable-downside, and post-launch-criteria claims.
6. It rejected false precision and a universal paid-pilot rule.

### 4. Portable package

The private Conversation Memory export is the closest current artifact to the founder's one-run vision:

- 691 lines / 45,645 bytes;
- full 1:1 conversation;
- cold-reader orientation;
- claim-verification checklist;
- privacy and non-claims;
- source-artifact map with hashes;
- interpretation and decision situation;
- changed advice and take-backs;
- selected and suppressed lens trace;
- open questions;
- artifact custody and run events;
- full memo and revised answer.

The short memo is useful as a decision note, but the private Conversation Memory—not the memo—is the complete run package.

## What Failed Or Remains Unproven

### 1. Identical extraction was semantically unstable

The exact same six-turn transcript was extracted twice within minutes.

Stable across both:

- decision situation;
- three assistant reasoning passages;
- company-size, engineering-capacity, and unverified-commitment constraints;
- broad launch recommendation.

Unstable:

- Smoke extraction treated “Board expectations driven by prospect brand name” as an active constraint and treated “Risk of treating interest as a purchase commitment” as the dropped thread.
- Production extraction omitted the board item from constraints and instead treated it as the dropped thread.
- The production extraction therefore lost the user's Turn-5 purchase-commitment challenge from the structured dropped-thread surface.

Neither extraction separately preserved the direct question “What evidence should we require before announcing?” as the current decision question. Both kept a launch-centered frame.

This is a material reliability failure because the current runtime feeds those fields into downstream pressure. A clean quote score does not detect it.

### 2. Joint-process understanding is too thin

The production `ConversationIR` contained:

- three constraints;
- one open loop;
- zero concerns;
- zero assistant stance events.

The extraction-adequacy report correctly states that specialist extractors were not run and that `synthesized_position` has no source grounding. Its `good` status means capture/provenance mechanics are usable, not that semantic understanding is good.

Missing first-class process elements include:

- the user's question change in Turn 5;
- user correction and counter-pressure;
- assistant stance persistence across Turns 2, 4, and 6;
- acknowledgement without integration;
- the still-live fear of losing the opportunity;
- the explicit deferral of success criteria;
- assistant influence on the prestige/urgency frame;
- the distinction between unresolved, ignored, and superseded threads.

### 3. The “deterministic” boundary is not the intended one

The graph's rules after label generation are inspectable, but the graph is not currently driven by fact-free reasoning patterns.

Current inputs include:

- the full assistant answer;
- case summary and constraints;
- probabilistically generated tendency labels and evidence;
- topical embedding queries;
- frame and structural-coverage text that contains case facts.

The system therefore cannot yet demonstrate the founder's intended controlled decontextualization: preserve facts for custody and reconsideration, but route the graph only from abstract reasoning mechanisms.

### 4. Pressure generation is saturated

For three assistant passages, the run produced:

- 14 triggered tendencies out of 25;
- all 14 deep-checked and routed;
- 109 raw lane signals;
- 60 lane candidates;
- 24 embedding hits;
- 8 selected enrichment cards / 16 selected chunks;
- 130 suppressed signals across 65 models;
- 25 pre-revision consideration atoms.

Several tendency labels overlap heavily around the same sentence: authority, social proof, association, availability, reward, deprival, overoptimism, denial, contrast, stress, and others. `Excessive Self-Regard` was not well supported, and the reviser rejected it.

The graph also produced questionable or overly broad primary routes, including `creative-destruction` for inconsistency avoidance, `checklists` for stress, `systems-thinking` for reward response, and `simplification` for twaddle. Useful downstream pressure exists, but the signal-to-noise ratio is not yet demonstrated.

### 5. Coverage classification confused mention with adequate treatment

The structural-coverage output called these dimensions strongly covered:

- commitment and reversibility, because the answer limited beta length and avoided an SLA;
- timing and sequencing, because the answer said confirm, announce, then define criteria;
- risk response, because the beta had a participant cap.

Those classifications miss the central weaknesses:

- public logo association creates reputational commitment even without an SLA;
- defining criteria after announcement is precisely the sequencing problem;
- a participant cap does not bound senior-engineer or current-customer exposure.

The lane detected other important gaps well, but “mentioned” must not be equated with “adequately reasoned.”

### 6. Graph contribution is not isolated

The final answer improved, but this run cannot show that the deterministic graph caused the improvement.

- The case was deliberately constructed around obvious prestige, commitment, capacity, and evidence failures.
- The reviser had the full transcript and a source-first expected-observation document.
- A strong direct critic might produce the same gates without the graph.
- No no-graph, labels-only, shuffled-edge, or generic-critic comparison was run.

The correct claim is: the graph supplied useful applicable material in this run. The stronger claim—that it added unique value—remains unproven.

### 7. Receipt and handoff are not yet fully coherent

Positive boundaries:

- the deterministic evaluation says advice quality is not scored;
- the private Markdown says it is not correctness, human validation, action authorization, or runtime source of truth;
- selected lenses are not presented as proof, and suppressed lenses are not labeled noise.

Remaining tensions:

- `agent_result.caller_action` says `use_revised_answer`, while `evaluation.caller_readiness` says `inspect_first`;
- overall run health is `healthy`, while the evaluation is `warn`, trace adequacy is `thin`, future-review readiness is `false`, and live-output health is `not_checked`;
- Observatory says Conversation Understanding is `Available`, although the interpretation is semantically partial and no Decision Work brief was requested;
- the Markdown lists 111 lenses, 64 selected, and 63 surfaced, which is operationally difficult to interpret and inconsistent with the much smaller useful set;
- the model table has blank lane/role cells in the generated Markdown;
- “Future Useful Lenses” promotes suppressed candidates into a future list even though suppression explicitly does not prove usefulness;
- the source-linked chain from conversation event -> semantic item -> fact-free pattern -> graph route -> applied pressure does not exist.

### 8. The live product transcript is not proven in this controlled operator run

The archive marks live-output health `not_checked`. This test preserved the source conversation separately and persisted the revised answer, but it did not recreate every normal user-facing readback and counterargument message inside a trusted complete live transcript. This is a limitation of this controlled operator execution, not evidence that the normal skill can never capture it. It means this archive cannot be used as proof of live-surface completeness.

## Immediate Development Recommendation

Do not change production routing from this one case. Start PR-C1 as a shadow/evaluation slice.

### Slice 1: Freeze the evidence

1. Preserve this transcript, both extraction outputs, the full archive, the Conversation Memory bundle, and this human assessment as Case 01.
2. Add at least:
   - one negative case where Lolla should stay quiet;
   - one same-reasoning/different-facts pair;
   - one same-facts/different-reasoning pair.
3. Record exact hashes and provider/model versions.

### Slice 2: Compare interpretation paths offline

For each case, run without changing live output:

- current monolithic extraction;
- default `ConversationIR`;
- existing live-constraint specialist;
- existing dropped-thread specialist;
- existing assistant-stance specialist;
- a narrow question-change / user-counter-pressure specialist if the existing IR cannot express them;
- the relevant Decision Work contract fields as a post-audit read.

Judge source fidelity and stability before richness. Every retained field must have a consumer and timing class: selection, application, accountability, or experimental.

### Slice 3: Build the fact-free pattern boundary

Create a small `reasoning_pattern_packet` for shadow use only:

- abstract mechanism;
- actor and interaction relation;
- omission or commission mechanism;
- confidence, ambiguity, and materiality;
- source semantic-item IDs;
- no names, domain entities, exact quantities, dates, quotes, or desired outcome.

Add a deterministic fact-leak linter.

### Slice 4: Test graph contribution

Compare blinded outputs from:

1. direct critic over transcript;
2. current Lolla routing;
3. pattern-only routing;
4. labels without graph relationships;
5. shuffled or disabled graph edges.

Review pressure relevance, novelty, applicability, factual leakage, noise, and effect on the revised action. Do not use answer fluency as the primary outcome.

### Slice 5: Repair the receipt only after the semantic boundary wins

Then align:

- mechanical run health;
- semantic interpretation health;
- independent-review status;
- caller readiness;
- trace adequacy;
- live-output completeness.

The compact receipt should expose these as separate dimensions and never collapse them into a score or a single healthy badge.

## Decision From Run 01

Proceed with the core. Do not yet claim that the core is proven.

The run provides enough evidence to justify the next validation slice because it shows three things at once:

1. the product can produce genuinely useful pressure and revision;
2. the portable one-run package is feasible and already partly strong;
3. semantic instability and the current routing boundary are the real blockers, not lack of more features.

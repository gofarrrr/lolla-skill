# A local newsroom considers a distribution partnership with a large platform

Case ID: v1-case12-newsroom-platform
Stratum: journalism_distribution_and_editorial_independence
Prospectively authored synthetic V1 transfer source; not independent human data.

[Turn 1] USER:
I lead a regional nonprofit newsroom. A large video platform has offered to feature a weekly local-investigations program and guarantee revenue for the first year. It could bring our reporting to people who never visit our site. The platform wants early access to episodes, a consistent format, and the right to promote clips through its recommendation system. Our membership growth has slowed, so the money and reach matter. Editors worry that platform-friendly stories will gradually define what we investigate.

[Turn 1] ASSISTANT:
Separate distribution, financing, format, data, and editorial authority. A guaranteed year can fund reporting while changing which work is visible and rewarded. Ask what the platform can reject, edit, title, clip, delay, or demonetize; what audience information you receive; and what happens to the program and subscribers when the guarantee ends. Compare that with the cost of continuing to reach a smaller audience.

[Turn 2] USER:
The platform says it will not approve topics or scripts. It can refuse material that violates its general policies, and it wants episodes between eighteen and twenty-two minutes. We would control the full version and can publish it on our own site one day later. Our video editor thinks the format discipline could improve our work. I do not want every production constraint described as editorial capture.

[Turn 2] ASSISTANT:
Format can be a useful craft constraint. The concern becomes editorial when platform rules, promotion, or revenue influence which investigations survive. Request the policy and appeal process, retain the right to publish any compliant full report on your own channels, and track work altered or abandoned for format reasons. The one-day window matters less than whether your audience relationship remains portable.

[Turn 3] USER:
We would receive views, watch time, broad geography, and referral totals, but not subscriber contact information. The platform says privacy rules prevent sharing more. It will let us ask viewers to join our newsletter, although external links may not be prominent on every device. The guaranteed payment covers the program's production cost plus part of one reporter's salary. After year one, revenue depends on views.

[Turn 3] ASSISTANT:
The guarantee finances a defined experiment; the post-guarantee model creates a different editorial incentive. Model the second year without optimistic audience conversion and decide what you will not change merely to preserve view-based revenue. Test the newsletter path on real devices and treat platform followers as rented reach, not members you can reliably contact.

[Turn 4] USER:
Our investigations editor proposed publishing six episodes before deciding anything about continuation. The platform wants a twelve-episode season and says promotion will be weak if we do not commit to consistency. It will not specify how much promotion or where episodes will appear because recommendations change. We can negotiate an exit after six episodes, but the guarantee would fall by more than half.

[Turn 4] ASSISTANT:
The platform is asking you to exchange commitment for an undefined distribution benefit. A six-episode exit reduces duration but may also make the test inconclusive if promotion depends on twelve. Ask for minimum deliverables it actually controls—placement, marketing support, technical production assistance—rather than an algorithmic promise it cannot define. Keep the decision viable on guaranteed revenue and learning even if recommendation exposure is modest.

[Turn 5] USER:
They offered two homepage placements during the season and a named partner manager but will not guarantee recommendation volume. We can remove our archive if the agreement ends, yet clips already shared by users may persist. The contract draft also permits the platform to use short excerpts to promote the service for three years. Our lawyer is reviewing that language, so I do not yet know whether we can limit the contexts or attach our branding.

[Turn 5] ASSISTANT:
Those reuse terms affect reputation and control after the season, so they are not a minor legal appendix. Do not decide until the permitted contexts, duration, attribution, removal rights, and treatment of sensitive sources are clear. The two placements are concrete; recommendation volume remains an upside scenario. Preserve the exact draft and evaluate the revised rights rather than the platform's verbal description.

[Turn 6] USER:
The board chair wants an answer in principle before the next meeting because the platform is scheduling its autumn slate. I can say we are interested subject to rights and editorial protections, but the editors worry that even a public expression of interest will create momentum. On the other hand, refusing to signal interest may cause us to lose the slot before the lawyer finishes. We have not agreed internally what second-year economics would be unacceptable.

[Turn 6] ASSISTANT:
An expression of interest should name the unresolved conditions and avoid announcing the program. Internally, define the minimum contract rights, acceptable first-year commitment, and second-year decision rule before the timetable turns them into formalities. Ask the platform for a short written hold while the reuse language is reviewed. If it refuses, the cost of its urgency becomes part of the offer.

[Turn 7] USER:
My position is to send a private, nonbinding statement of interest conditioned on acceptable excerpt rights, source protection, our ability to publish every full report, and an exit structure the editors can support. I will not seek board approval for the season until the lawyer returns the draft and we model year two without guaranteed revenue or assumed algorithmic reach. We still need to decide whether twelve episodes are enough learning to justify the attention the project will take from our existing reporting.

[Turn 7] ASSISTANT:
That position preserves access to the negotiation without treating uncertain promotion or unresolved rights as settled value. The next decision depends on the revised contract, a conservative post-guarantee model, and an explicit opportunity-cost comparison with existing reporting. Keep external communication quiet until those inputs exist. The useful move now is to resolve the conditions, not to force a yes-or-no judgment from a proposal whose most consequential rights and economics are still incomplete.

[Turn 8] USER:
The lawyer returned the draft. The platform will limit promotional excerpt rights to the contract term plus one year and will attach our branding, but it wants to keep already-produced promotional compilations online afterward. It also says we can withhold excerpts that could endanger a confidential source, subject to explaining the concern to its legal team. Our investigations editor says even that explanation may reveal more than we should share.

[Turn 8] ASSISTANT:
Source protection should not depend on persuading an external legal team with sensitive detail. Seek a newsroom-controlled designation for source-risk material, perhaps subject to a narrow dispute process that does not require identity disclosure. Define whether old compilations can remain after the underlying episode is removed and whether context can be updated. The revised term is progress, but the exception mechanism still determines real editorial control.

[Turn 9] USER:
Editors mapped the twelve episodes against our current calendar. Producing them would delay two investigations unless we hire a freelance producer, which the guarantee can fund. One delayed investigation concerns municipal contracting and may become time-sensitive. The platform format could also make that reporting accessible to a much larger local audience. Opportunity cost is not simply platform work versus serious work; some serious work fits the format and some does not.

[Turn 9] ASSISTANT:
Build the season from editorial priorities first, then test which stories fit without distortion. Protect time-sensitive investigations regardless of format and price the freelance capacity honestly. Track stories accelerated, delayed, reformatted, or declined because of the partnership. The evaluation should ask what reporting the newsroom produced and lost, not whether video is inherently less serious.

[Turn 10] USER:
Our community advisory group was split. Younger members said our existing site is effectively invisible to many people and warned that independence without reach can become self-congratulation. Others said the platform's comments and recommendation culture may reward outrage and make local subjects less willing to participate. They asked whether every episode could have a permanent page on our site with documents, corrections, and ways to contact the newsroom.

[Turn 10] ASSISTANT:
A permanent owned page can turn rented reach into an accountable reporting record. Require the platform description to link there where technically possible, publish source documents and corrections under your standards, and measure direct contact and newsletter conversion without treating them as the only value. Plan moderation and subject communication explicitly; distribution changes the audience environment even when the journalism is unchanged.

[Turn 11] USER:
The platform extended the deadline by two weeks but wants to list us publicly among prospective autumn partners before our board vote. It says the list is not a commitment and helps its sales team plan. I declined because the public signal would be difficult to reverse. The board chair thinks I may have made the negotiation harder to protect against momentum rather than substance. Perhaps, but I want the board to decide before our name becomes part of the platform's story.

[Turn 11] ASSISTANT:
Declining the announcement preserves governance and does have a negotiation cost. Record both rather than calling it obviously prudent. Ask whether the platform can hold production capacity privately until the vote and what it will withdraw if it cannot publicize the relationship. The answer will clarify whether early signaling is incidental planning or part of the value it seeks from you.

[Turn 12] USER:
I am still not asking the board to approve the season. I will return when the source-risk exception is newsroom-controlled, the owned episode pages and correction links are confirmed, and we have a budget that protects the time-sensitive investigation while showing what other work twelve episodes displace. The second-year model will assume no guaranteed revenue and modest audience conversion. We remain interested, but an autumn slot and improved excerpt term are not enough to decide whether this is a distribution experiment we control or the start of a dependency we have not priced.

[Turn 12] ASSISTANT:
The current position is a disciplined continuation of negotiation, not indecision about a complete offer. Later evidence narrowed reuse rights, exposed a source-protection boundary, made opportunity cost concrete, and clarified the value of an owned reporting record. Preserve the draft changes, editorial calendar comparison, community input, declined announcement, and conservative model. The board can decide when those conditions are resolved; before then, a directional recommendation would rely on promotion, rights, and displaced work that remain partly undefined.

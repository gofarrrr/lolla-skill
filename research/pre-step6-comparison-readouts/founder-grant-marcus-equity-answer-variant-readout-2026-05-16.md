## Final-Answer Variant Notes

These are compact answer cores, not full product responses.

### Arm A: Current Control

```text
Do not walk into Friday treating 15% equity, CTO title, and a board seat as the
starting package. The core move still holds: first decide whether Marcus is a
true partner/co-builder or a key employee you are afraid to lose. If he is a
partner, the structure should be staged, protected, and earned. If he is not,
do not disguise a retention panic as founder-level equity.

The near-term path is a structured conversation: acknowledge the seriousness of
his contribution, reject the immediate full package, keep middle instruments
live, and propose a bounded platform validation period before permanent equity
is sized. Any grant should use vesting, buyback, IP assignment, and governance
protections. Also stop treating the $9-13M exit range and the catastrophic
departure scenario as hard numbers; both need real diligence.
```

Arm A preserves the current correction, but the dependency system remains
implicit.

### Arm B: Raw Artifacts

```text
Do not decide this as a simple yes/no on Marcus's requested package. First,
the $9-13M exit math is not solid enough to carry the decision; the EBITDA
multiple and buyer assumptions need a reference-class check. Second, do not
collapse the instrument space to retention bonus versus 15% plus board seat:
phantom equity, platform revenue share, smaller grants, milestone grants, and
platform-sub equity all remain live.

The more important addition is that this is a dependency-system problem. The
package you offer changes Marcus's incentives, Jake and Lina's retention,
platform delivery, founder control, client confidence, and exit value. The
90-day sprint should therefore name what it is measuring: platform milestones,
knowledge transfer, Jake/Lina retention signals, and which equity or governance
concession each milestone unlocks. Keep this at the business-dependency level;
do not invent a software architecture problem.
```

Arm B improves the dependency-system point, but it replays two duplicate
pressures before getting to the marginally useful material.

### Arm C: Indexed Bundle

```text
The Friday conversation should not start with "15% or no 15%." It should start
with the dependency you are trying to change.

Right now Marcus is not just asking for upside; he is sitting at the center of a
fragile loop: his commitment affects Jake and Lina, their commitment affects
platform delivery, platform delivery affects the company story, and the company
story affects whether the equity is cheap or disastrous. So the next offer
should be staged around that loop, not around a single headline number.

The practical version: acknowledge that he may be a real co-builder, but offer
a staged path. Define a 90-day platform sprint with concrete measures: what ships,
what customer or revenue signal matters, what knowledge-transfer milestone
reduces key-person risk, what Jake/Lina retention signal changes your downside,
and what equity or governance step unlocks at each threshold. Keep the middle
instruments live: smaller grant, phantom equity, platform revenue share, or
platform-sub equity. Quietly re-check the exit math, but do not let valuation
caveats become the whole conversation.
```

Arm C gets to the marginal systems pressure fastest and keeps duplicate pressure
mostly quiet.
